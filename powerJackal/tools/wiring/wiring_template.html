Changed in this revision. U1 is now TI's TPSM53602 module, set to 5.0 V by R3 and R4. U4 is now an ESP32-C3-MINI-1U-N4X, so USB-C flashes and prints over the chip's built-in USB serial and JTAG. GPIO9 gets a pull-up (R23), and USB D+ and D− get 22 Ω series resistors (R24, R25). The board is 72.4 × 47.0 mm with the same VEX hole pattern.

0Overview

Top row: smart port or bench supply to 5 V. Bottom row: 5 V (or USB) to 3.3 V. Three INA226 chips tap across the shunts, and the ESP32-C3 reads all three over I2C.

1Inputs and fuse

J1 and J2 each feed VIN_OR through their own Schottky diode, so either input can be used alone or both at once. F1 makes VFUSE, and after the input shunt everything sits on VIN_MOD.

J1's footprint is still a stand-in. Match the pins by wire color, then confirm on a powered brain with a multimeter before you order boards: battery voltage should show between the yellow and green contacts.

2TPSM53602 step-down

The module has the switches and inductor inside and runs at a fixed 1.4 MHz. R3 and R4 set 5.0 V, from Table 7-1 of the TPSM53602 datasheet.

  • C2, C3 (2 × 10 µF 50 V) and C8 sit right at the VIN pads. C4, C5 (2 × 47 µF) sit right at the VOUT pads.
  • EN is tied to VIN, which the datasheet allows. The module starts at its internal UVLO.
  • DNC pins 4 and 5 and V5V (pin 13) stay unconnected. NC pins 3, 10, 11 and AGND (12) run into the exposed pad, which is where AGND meets PGND.
  • The exposed pad has 11 thermal vias into the bottom ground plane. They are covered on the back so solder doesn't wick through.
  • Pads sit under the body: solder it with a stencil and a hot plate or hot air.

35 V current sense

U2 measures what the inputs supply, U3 measures what the 5 V rail delivers. Same chip, different nets and address.

  • IN+ and IN− run as their own thin traces from the inside edges of the shunt pads (Kelvin connection), never from the power copper.

43.3 V regulator and its test

U6 makes 3V3 from 5V_LDO, which is fed by the 5 V rail through D2 or by USB through D4. U5 measures the 3.3 V voltage and current. J9 brings 3.3 V out for a plug-in load or a test sensor.

  • D2 and D4 stop the 5 V rail and USB from feeding into each other. The LDO input sits around 4.6 V, well above the 3.64 V the AP7361C needs at full load.
  • The test: read U5 with nothing on J9, then plug in the 22 Ω load to add 150 mA. Check how far 3V3 drops at TP4 and scope it for ripple.
  • The U6 tab is GND and sits in the ground pour.

5ESP32-C3 and USB-C

The ESP32-C3-MINI-1U module has the chip, 4 MB of flash and the crystal inside. Its antenna connector stays empty because the radio isn't used. USB is built into the chip on GPIO18 and GPIO19, so USB-C flashes the firmware and carries the serial terminal with no USB-to-serial chip and no drivers.

  • Flashing: plug in USB-C and upload from Arduino, PlatformIO or ESP-IDF. If a firmware bug ever breaks USB, hold BOOT (SW2), tap RESET (SW1), let go of BOOT. The chip then sits in its ROM download mode, which always works over USB.
  • Arduino: pick "ESP32C3 Dev Module", set USB CDC On Boot to Enabled, and call Wire.begin(4, 5) for I2C (SDA GPIO4, SCL GPIO5). Status LED is GPIO10, PGOOD is GPIO3.
  • GPIO2, GPIO8 and GPIO9 are boot pins. R21, R22 and R23 hold them high. Connect nothing else to them.
  • Keep Wi-Fi and Bluetooth off in firmware. No antenna is fitted.
  • R14 and R15 must be two separate 5.1 kΩ resistors. Without them a USB-C to USB-C cable won't turn on 5 V.
  • Q1 switches USB power by itself. With no port or bench power, USB runs the 3.3 V side so you can flash on the desk. Once the 5 V rail is up, Q1 turns off and USB only carries data, so the port readings never include USB current. Nothing to cut or flip.
  • On USB power alone the TPSM53602 is off, and TI only specifies PGOOD with VIN at 2 V or more. R7 will most likely pull it high, so check U2's bus voltage in firmware before you trust PGOOD.
  • The module footprint and symbol come from Espressif's official KiCad library. Its pads sit under the module, so use a stencil and a hot plate or hot air.

6Plug-in loads (off board)

Each load is a Phoenix 1803578 plug with a cement resistor's leads clamped in its two screws. Plug it into J6 for 5 V or J9 for 3.3 V. Nothing hot sits on the board.
